Have you ever had burning/tingling pain or electric shooting pain into the toes? One cause for this could be Morton's Neuroma. A Morton's Neuroma is an accumulation of scar tissue around the nerve bundle between the 3rd and 4th interdigital space of your toes. Located below the deep transverse ligament, this condition mainly affects women and can be caused by these 4 factors: 1) Inflammation near neuroma (possibly due to intermetatarsal bursiitis 2) chronic traction damage 3) compression of the deep transverse intermetatarsal ligament 4) Ischemia of vasa nervorum (which are small blood vessels that supply blood to peripheral nerves). There are multiple ways to diagnose this condition. Clinical tests includes: 1) Gauthier's Test, 2) Mulder's click test 3) Thumb-index finger fqueeze test. A more invasive clinical diagnostic method includes a local Lidocaine injection to the ID space, where if the patient's pain immediately subscides, then a diagnosis of Morton's neuroma is highly likely. Additionally, Ultrasound imaging can have a 90-95% sensitivity if done by experienced radiologist. If the Imaging tests are negative, common differential diagnosis include intermetatarsal bursiitis, MPJ capsulitis, plantar plate tear, and metatarsal stress fracture. Moving on to treatment options, if we start with a conservative approach, this includes using orthoses and footwear to offload the nerve. Most Chiropodist/Podiatrist will recommend a wide & deep toe box Rocker Bottom shoe and construct an orthotic with a movable met pad to create space between the ID space. How successful is this treatment method? Well a study by S. Valisena, et al., found 48% success rate in pain relief with the use of orthoses for patients after a 6 month follow up. However, the study also notes that operative treatments had a 89% success rate and infiltrative came as a close second with a 85% success rate (SR). Infiltrative treatment includes radio-frequency ablation (81%), alcohol injections (71% SR), cortisone injection (CS) (51% SR). Operative treatment includes a neurectomy (88% SR) and decompression surgery (94% SR). This review shows that operative and infiltrative treatments offer the best outcomes. The comparison per study design highlights that surgery has better outcomes than infiltration. The complication rate is higher with surgery, while the recurrence rate is higher with both infiltrative and conservative treatments. It was suprising to see how CS injections demonstrated a similar efficacy to orthotics. This is a very common treatment recommendation where chiropodist/Podiatrist would use a combination of Celestone or Kenalog with Lidocaine (normally at a ratio of 1:2). Presentation by Jonathan Haslehurst BA, DCh & Ruth Avelino BSc, DCh stated that If a neuroma id under 5mm in size then a CS injection can provide a 85 % success rate. But if over 5mm in size, then it can drop down to 59%. Where 6.3mm if the cut-off point.
